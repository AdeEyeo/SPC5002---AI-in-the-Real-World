Web (1,900) and phone (96) orders make 1,996 orders. The CRM extract has 1,088 rows for 1,061 customers, because 27 customers appear twice. Joined to the full CRM, the orders became 2,030 rows. Joined with `validate="many_to_one"` to a CRM with one row per customer, they stay at 1,996.

A left join copies each order once for every CRM row matching its customer, so a customer with two CRM rows has all their orders doubled. The extra rows therefore equal the orders those customers placed, not the number of customers: 20 of the 27 ordered once and 7 ordered twice, giving 20 + 14 = 34.

I kept neither row for these 27 customers, so their 34 orders keep their order data but have blank region, tenure and opt-in until the CRM owner says which record is right. Keeping the first row would let whichever record sits higher in the spreadsheet win, and the pairs disagree on tenure in all 27 cases and on region in 24. I would rather leave a blank, which announces itself, than a value that may be false and looks certain. The cost is losing values both rows agree on, such as opt-in for 25 customers. I would ask the CRM owner how one customer came to have two records, which one is current, and whether the extract is meant to hold one row per customer.
