# Speaker grid

Every guest on the module is asked the same six questions, and the seventh is
for you alone. There are four guests this term, in Weeks 3, 6, 9 and 12, and in
the spring you choose a project partly from what is written here.

Write each answer in your own words while it is fresh, the same day.

## Week 3, Friday 9 October

Speaker: Shiyamalaraj Kandasamy - PhD Candidate, Lecturer in Computing
Organisation: School of Computing, University of Buckingham

1. What is your field?

Data science, quality engineering, operation management, computing

2. How do you use machine learning?

Defect Prediction, Quality Prediction, Anomaly Prediction, Root Cause Analysis

3. What data do you use, and how do you clean it?

Serial number, Material properties, Machines used, Operators, Outcome, Events during and after production (e.g., tool break or alarm), Time stamps.

Process mining to derive intended paths of each individual. Actual paths are compared with its ideal path to quantify the deviation, each serial number now has a single "real-world performance number" attached for downstream work. Extracts a sequential dataset from results of process mining.
Clustering applied to find two peaks for the time between repeated serial numbers, each corresponds to one of rerun or rework. Used a mix of logics and arithmetics to classify the repeated records as either rerun or rework. The logical check says if two identical serial number appears consecutively, it was a rerun. The arithmetic check calculates the mean gap time between every repeated records and class the records as rerun if its gap time falls below that mean.

4. What does your pipeline look like?

Manufacture pipepline: Many Serial numbers -> one Job card -> Many Shifts -> Many Machines and Many Operators -> Many Breakdowns and Services

5. How do you check your results, and change what you do?

Calculate Accuracy, True Positive/Negative rate and F1 score. Found an extreme class imbalance. Plotted a graph of the distance between each data point and saw that almost all piled on top of each other even at high resolution.

Plotted the impact on quality by the different recorded events on a graph to capture anomalies.

6. Who do you have to explain all this to?

Flintec company

7. Could I see myself doing this work, and why?

No, even though the work presented in the case study is familiar to the practice projects we have done and I feel comfortable do these work. The presenter mentioned a  general lack of data and proprietary issues. The quality and quantity of the data is mandatory to produce sufficient results in data science, so I am worried of the consequences to face if the applied model failed and caused a loss for the company. Despite that, the manufacture pipeline may generally lack hardware that records effective data, which seems unresolvable to me.
